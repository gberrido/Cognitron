# Black Holes

## What They Are
Black holes are regions of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape from them. They are predicted by Einstein's general theory of relativity and are characterized by an event horizon, the point of no return.

## How They Form
Black holes form through several mechanisms:

- **Stellar Collapse**: When massive stars (≥ 20 M☉) exhaust nuclear fuel, their cores collapse under gravity, forming a **stellar‑mass black hole**.
- **Binary Mergers**: Two compact objects (e.g., neutron stars) can merge, creating a black hole of intermediate mass.
- **Direct Collapse**: In the early universe, massive gas clouds can collapse directly into **super‑massive black holes** without a star intermediate.

## Properties
- **Event Horizon**: The boundary beyond which nothing escapes. Its radius is the **Schwarzschild radius**: \(r_s = 2GM/c^2\).
- **Singularity**: A point (or ring for rotating black holes) where density and curvature become infinite.
- **Mass, Spin, Charge**: According to the “no‑hair” theorem, a black hole is fully described by mass, angular momentum (spin), and electric charge.
- **Time Dilation**: Time runs slower near the event horizon; an external observer sees infalling matter freeze at the horizon.
- **Hawking Radiation**: Quantum effects cause black holes to emit a faint thermal radiation, leading to eventual evaporation for small black holes.

## Recent Discoveries
- **Event Horizon Telescope (EHT)**: In 2019, the EHT produced the first direct image of a super‑massive black hole (M87*). In 2022, the first image of the black hole at the center of our Milky Way, **Sgr A***, was released.
- **Gravitational Wave Observations**: LIGO/Virgo have detected dozens of binary black‑hole mergers, revealing a population of ~10–100 M☉ black holes and confirming predictions of general relativity.
- **Intermediate‑Mass Black Holes**: In 2023, evidence for a ~150 M☉ black hole in the dwarf galaxy RGG 118 was reported, filling the gap between stellar and super‑massive black holes.
- **Black Hole Echoes**: Recent studies have searched for subtle “echoes” in gravitational‑wave signals that could hint at quantum structure at the horizon, a topic of ongoing debate.

These findings continue to deepen our understanding of black holes and their role in the evolution of galaxies and the universe.
