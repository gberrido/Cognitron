# Ancient Greek Civilization

## Overview
Ancient Greece (c. 1100 – 146 BC) was a civilization that flourished in the Mediterranean region, particularly on the mainland of Greece, the Aegean islands, and the western coast of Anatolia. It is renowned for its contributions to philosophy, science, art, politics, and literature, which have profoundly shaped Western civilization.

## Historical Periods
- **Archaic (c. 800–480 BC)**: Formation of city‑states (poleis) such as Athens, Sparta, and Corinth; development of the alphabet and early poetry.
- **Classical (c. 480–323 BC)**: Height of political and cultural achievement; democracy in Athens; the Persian Wars; the Golden Age of Athens under Pericles.
- **Hellenistic (323–146 BC)**: Expansion of Greek culture after the conquests of Alexander the Great; blending of Greek and Eastern cultures; major centers in Alexandria, Pergamon, and Seleucia.

## Society and Politics
- **City‑states (Polis)**: Independent political entities with their own governments, laws, and customs. The most famous examples are democratic Athens and militaristic Sparta.
- **Democracy**: Developed in Athens around 508 BC, allowing free male citizens to participate directly in decision‑making through assemblies and councils.
- **Social Structure**: Citizens (free adult males), metics (resident foreigners), and slaves. Women had limited public roles but could own property in some city‑states.

## Culture and Religion
- **Religion**: Polytheistic, worshipping a pantheon led by Zeus. Rituals, festivals (e.g., the Olympic Games), and oracles (e.g., Delphi) were central.
- **Philosophy**: Home to seminal thinkers—Socrates, Plato, Aristotle—who laid the foundations of Western philosophy and scientific methodology.
- **Literature**: Epic poetry (Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey*), tragedy (Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides), and comedy (Aristophanes). 
- **Art and Architecture**: Development of the three classical orders—Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian. Notable works include the Parthenon, sculptures like the *Venus de Milo*, and the use of marble and bronze.

## Science and Technology
- **Mathematics**: Pythagoras, Euclid, Archimedes—foundations of geometry, number theory, and engineering.
- **Science**: Aristotle’s works on biology, physics, and logic; Hippocrates’ contributions to medicine.
- **Technology**: Advances in shipbuilding, the water screw (Archimedes' screw), and early mechanical devices.

## Legacy
- **Language**: Ancient Greek language, with its dialects (Attic, Doric, Ionic), became the basis for later forms such as Koine and modern Greek.
- **Influence**: The political concepts of democracy, citizenship, and law continue to inform modern political systems. Literary and philosophical traditions remain central to education and cultural studies worldwide.
